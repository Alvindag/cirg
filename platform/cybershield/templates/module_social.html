Social Engineering at Reception
Physical security and human manipulation — how attackers gain access to DAS Plc premises by exploiting helpfulness and trust.

🎭 What Is Social Engineering?

Social engineering manipulates people into giving up confidential information or physical access. Attackers exploit our desire to be helpful, our fear of authority, and our tendency to trust confident people.

  • Impersonation — pretending to be a vendor, colleague, or authority figure
  • Tailgating — following someone through a secure door without badge access
  • Pretexting — fabricating a scenario ("I'm here to fix the server")
  • Baiting — leaving something tempting (the USB drive in the car park)
  • Quid pro quo — offering a service in exchange for access ("free IT support")

DAS Plc's reception desk is the first and most important physical security checkpoint.

🏢 Scenario: The Kaspersky Visitor

It is Monday morning. You are at the DAS Plc reception desk. A smartly dressed man arrives and says:

"Good morning. I'm Daniel from Kaspersky Technical Support. We received alerts from your company's Kaspersky Security Center about critical infections. I need to access your server room to check the console directly — it will only take 15 minutes."

He shows a Kaspersky-branded business card and seems professional. What do you do?

📋 DAS Plc Visitor Management Protocol

  • All external visitors must be pre-registered with the department they are visiting
  • Unannounced visitors must wait while IT (Ext. 204) or the relevant manager is contacted
  • Visitors must sign the visitor log, show valid ID, and wear a visitor badge at all times
  • Visitors must be escorted by a DAS Plc employee at all times — never left alone
  • No visitor should access server rooms or IT infrastructure without IT approval
  • If in doubt — call Kaspersky's official number yourself, not the number the visitor gives you

🚨 Red Flags in the Kaspersky Scenario

  • No prior appointment — legitimate vendors schedule visits in advance
  • Requesting server room access on arrival — Kaspersky manages security remotely
  • Creating urgency — "critical infections" to pressure quick access
  • Business card is not verification — anyone can print a business card
  • Kaspersky Security Center is administered remotely — no physical server visit needed

🚪 Tailgating — The Silent Threat

Tailgating is when an unauthorised person follows a legitimate employee through a secured door without using their own access credentials. People feel rude "blocking" someone — attackers exploit this.

DAS Plc Tailgating Rules:
  • Every person entering a secured area must badge in with their own credentials
  • It is NOT rude to ask someone to badge in — it is your responsibility
  • If someone says "my badge isn't working", direct them to reception — do not hold the door
  • Report any unusual access attempts to IT (Ext. 204) immediately
