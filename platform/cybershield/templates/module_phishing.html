🎣 What Is Phishing?

Phishing is an attack where criminals send emails pretending to be a trusted person or organisation — your bank, IT department, a supplier, or even your CEO. Their goal is to:

  • Steal your Microsoft 365 login credentials (username and password)
  • Trick you into approving a fraudulent payment through Business Central
  • Install ransomware that encrypts all DAS Plc files and stops production
  • Access DAS Plc systems through your compromised account
  • Steal manufacturing formulas, supplier data, or financial records

Phishing is the number one attack vector against organisations like DAS Plc. Business Email Compromise (BEC) — a type of phishing targeting Finance teams — costs Ghanaian businesses hundreds of thousands of cedis every year.

🔴 The 8 Warning Signs — Memorise These

1. Sender domainDoes not match the real organisation (check after the @)
2. Urgency"Act NOW", "Account expires in 24 hours", "URGENT"
3. Generic greeting"Dear Valued Employee" — IT knows your name from Active Directory
4. Suspicious linksHover — if the real URL differs from the display text, it's a trap
5. Requesting credentialsIT will NEVER ask for your password by email. Ever.
6. Unexpected attachmentsEspecially .exe, .zip, .docm files from unknown senders
7. Unusual send time3am emails from "IT" — attackers work outside business hours
8. Wrong domain in linksLink shows "dasplcgh.com" but actually goes to "das-secure-portal.net"

📋 How to Report Phishing in Microsoft Outlook

At DAS Plc, reporting a phishing email is just as important as not clicking it. Every report feeds into our ISMS Phishing Intelligence system and protects the other 200+ staff. Here are your two methods:

✅ Method 1 — Outlook Report Message Button (Recommended)

This is the fastest way and automatically notifies Microsoft's security systems too.

1
Select the suspicious email in your inbox
Click once on the email to select it — do NOT open it or click any links inside it.
2
Find the "Report" button in the Home ribbon
In Outlook desktop: Look at the top ribbon (Home tab) → Find the "Report" or "Report Message" button. In Outlook Web App: Right-click the email in the list, or use the "…" (More actions) menu at the top of the reading pane.
3
Click "Report Message" → Select "Phishing"
A dropdown will appear with options: Junk, Phishing, Not Junk. Select "Phishing" (not just Junk). This sends the email to Microsoft for analysis AND flags it in our ISMS Phishing Intelligence system.
4
Confirm the report when prompted
A confirmation dialog may appear. Click "Report" to confirm. The email will be moved to your Deleted Items or Junk folder automatically.
5
Call IT to give verbal notification
For emails that look targeted (e.g. they use your name, mention DAS Plc systems specifically), also call Ext. 204 to inform IT directly. This triggers a faster investigation.

📩 Method 2 — Forward to IT Helpdesk

Use this method if the Report Message button is not available, or if you want IT to investigate a specific email urgently.

1
Do NOT click Reply — use "Forward as Attachment"
In Outlook: Go to Home → More → "Forward as Attachment". This sends the full email including all headers (critical for IT forensic analysis). A standard Forward removes header metadata.
2
Address it to IT helpdesk
Send to: IThelpdesk@dasplcgh.com or servicedesk@dasplcgh.com. Add a brief subject like: "Suspected phishing — please investigate".
3
Delete the original email
Once forwarded, delete the original from your inbox and empty your Deleted Items to prevent accidental clicks later.
🚫 NEVER do these things with a phishing email:
  • Click any link or attachment inside the email
  • Reply to the sender asking them to stop
  • Forward it to colleagues to "warn them" — use the proper reporting channel
  • Delete it without reporting — reporting protects all 200+ DAS Plc staff

💼 Phishing Targeting Microsoft 365 Business Central

Business Central (BC) is now DAS Plc's core ERP system managing purchase orders, supplier payments, financial approvals, and production data. This makes it a high-value target. Criminals specifically craft phishing attacks designed to steal BC credentials or trick Finance staff into approving fraudulent payments.

🎯 BC Phishing Attack Types — What to Watch For

🔴 Fake BC Login Pages
You receive an email saying "Your Business Central session has expired — click here to log back in." The link goes to a fake BC login page that looks identical to Microsoft's — but steals your M365 credentials. DAS Plc will never send you a BC login link by email. Always navigate to BC by typing the URL directly.
🔴 Fake BC Payment Approval Emails
An email arrives looking like a BC notification: "Supplier payment GHS 120,000 pending your approval — click here to review." The link goes to a fake BC portal that either steals your credentials or tricks you into "approving" a payment to the attacker's account. Real BC approvals happen INSIDE Business Central only — never via external email links.
🔴 Fake Supplier Invoices with BC References
A supplier email claims "Please update our bank account in your BC system for future payments" and includes a fake invoice with new bank details. This is fraud. Never change supplier bank details based on an email alone. Always verify by calling the supplier on their known official number — not the number in the email.
🔴 BEC — CEO/MD Requesting BC Payment
WhatsApp or email appearing to be from the MD/CEO: "I'm in a meeting — urgently process GHS 85,000 payment to [new supplier] in BC before close of business." This is Business Email Compromise. Financial instructions via WhatsApp or email require mandatory phone verification — no exceptions.

✅ How to Stay Safe in Business Central

✅ Always access BC directly
Log into Business Central by navigating directly to the BC URL in your browser — never through a link in an email or Teams message. Bookmark the real BC URL.
✅ Verify approvals in BC itself
If you receive an email claiming a BC approval is pending, ignore the email link and log directly into BC. Check the Approvals section inside BC. If nothing is there, the email was fake.
✅ Verify ALL supplier bank detail changes by phone
Any request to change a supplier's bank account details in BC — whether by email, WhatsApp, or letter — must be verified by calling the supplier on their established known phone number. Not the number provided in the request.
✅ Report any suspicious BC-related emails immediately
If you receive a suspicious email referencing Business Central, forward it to IThelpdesk@dasplcgh.com and call Ext. 204 immediately. Early reporting has stopped real attacks at DAS Plc.
